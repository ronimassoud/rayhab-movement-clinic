Pillar 02 · The Schroth Method

What the Schroth Method actually involves

People arrive expecting exercises. What Schroth actually teaches is a correction, and then how to hold it while you do things.

Reading time3 min read
Written byRayan Halimeh, Certified Schroth Practitioner
StatusAwaiting clinical review

In short

  • Schroth is a curve-specific approach: the exercises are chosen for your pattern, not for scoliosis in general.
  • The core skill is producing an active correction yourself, not being put into position by someone else.
  • Rotational breathing is used to direct air into the flattened part of the chest.
  • It requires regular home practice. That is the part that decides whether it works.

Where it comes from

The method originated with Katharina Schroth in Germany in the 1920s and was developed further by her daughter Christa Lehnert-Schroth. It was built on the idea that a three-dimensional deformity needs a three-dimensional correction, held actively by the person themselves.

The working principles

  • Auto-elongation, actively lengthening the spine to reduce the curve before anything else is attempted.
  • De-rotation, turning the trunk against the direction of the curve's rotation.
  • Rotational breathing, directing breath into the concave, collapsed areas to expand them from the inside.
  • Stabilisation, holding the corrected position with muscular effort so it is not purely passive.

Those four are the grammar of the method. Individual exercises are sentences built from them.

What a session actually looks like

Early sessions are largely about learning to feel and produce the correction, often in supported positions using a wall, a bar, poles or a mat, with plenty of tactile and verbal cueing and frequent use of a mirror. It is slow, detailed work and it is more mentally demanding than most people expect.

As the correction becomes reliable, it is progressed: held for longer, in less supported positions, then during movement, then under load, and finally during the things you actually do.

The home programme is the treatment

Clinic sessions teach and refine. The home programme is where the volume happens, and it is usually the single biggest determinant of whether anything changes. A realistic programme performed most days beats an ambitious one performed occasionally.

This is why a good programme is short enough to survive a school week, and why it is reviewed and trimmed rather than only added to.

What to expect, honestly

Postural change and better control of the trunk are the things most people notice first, often within weeks. Changes in the measured angle, where they occur, take considerably longer and are not guaranteed. Anyone promising a specific number of degrees is overstating what is known.

What is not settled

The strongest evidence for curve-specific exercise concerns growing adolescents with modest curves, and even there the certainty of the evidence is limited. Its effect in adults, and its effect on large curves, is much less clear.
