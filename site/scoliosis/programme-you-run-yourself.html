Pillar 03 · Rayhab's approach

Why the programme is built to be handed over

Scoliosis is managed over years. Any plan that depends on you being in a clinic twice a week for those years is not a plan.

Reading time2 min read
Written byRayan Halimeh, Schroth Level 2
StatusAwaiting clinical review

In short

  • The programme is designed from the first session to be run by you at home.
  • Clinic sessions teach, correct and progress. They are not where the volume happens.
  • Independence is treated as an outcome and reviewed like any other.
  • Periodic review replaces indefinite attendance.

Start from the timescale

A curve found at thirteen is managed through a growth spurt, into skeletal maturity, and then for the rest of adult life. That is a horizon measured in years and decades, not in a block of six sessions.

Once you accept that timescale, a lot follows. Whatever is prescribed has to be sustainable without supervision, affordable in time, and robust enough to survive exams, holidays, injuries and periods of low motivation.

What clinic time is actually for

  • Teaching the correction, which needs hands and feedback and cannot be learned from a sheet.
  • Correcting drift, because every home programme decays in predictable ways.
  • Progressing, adding difficulty, load or duration once something is reliable.
  • Re-measuring, so changes are recorded rather than remembered.

None of those require weekly attendance indefinitely. They require attendance at the right moments.

Designing for handover

A programme built to be handed over looks different from one built to be supervised. It is shorter. It uses equipment you actually have. It has a clearly marked minimum version for bad weeks. And it is written down in a form you can follow when you have forgotten what the cue meant.

A programme that takes forty minutes and needs a studio will be abandoned, and an abandoned programme has no effect at all. A shorter programme that is actually done beats a better one that is not.

Independence as an outcome

At review the questions are not only about the curve. Can you produce the correction without cueing? Can you tell when you have lost it? Do you know which part of the programme to drop first when the week collapses, and which part never to drop?

If the answer to those is yes, the programme has been handed over successfully, whatever the angle is doing.
