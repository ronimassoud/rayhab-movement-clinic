Pillar 03 · Rayhab's approach

Working alongside a brace

Families are often presented with bracing and exercise as a choice. In current practice they are usually combined.

Reading time2 min read
Written byRayan Halimeh, Schroth Level 2
StatusAwaiting clinical review

In short

  • Bracing has the strongest evidence of any non-surgical treatment for reducing progression to surgical thresholds.
  • Wear time is the main determinant of whether a brace works.
  • Exercise alongside bracing helps maintain strength and mobility and supports the correction out of the brace.
  • The two are coordinated, not opposed.

What bracing is for

A brace does not straighten a spine permanently. Its purpose is to reduce the chance that a growing curve progresses to the point where surgery is considered. It is a growth-window intervention, which is why it is prescribed for skeletally immature patients.

The evidence here is stronger than for most things in scoliosis care. A landmark randomised trial found bracing significantly reduced progression to the surgical threshold compared with observation, and found a clear relationship between hours worn and success.

Wear time is the variable that matters

That same relationship is the most useful thing a family can know. More hours produced better outcomes, with substantial benefit at high daily wear. Prescriptions in the range of 18 to 23 hours a day are common, and results fall away as wear falls.

This makes adherence, not brace design, the usual difference between a brace that works and one that does not.

Where exercise fits

  • Maintaining strength and mobility in a trunk that spends most of the day supported.
  • Practising the correction out of the brace, so the shape is actively held rather than only externally imposed.
  • Breathing work, since a brace restricts the ribcage and chest mobility deserves attention.
  • Keeping the person moving, which matters for morale as much as for the spine.

International guidance supports combining scoliosis-specific exercise with bracing rather than choosing between them.

The practical side

Corrective exercises are usually performed out of the brace, during the daily time it is off. That time is limited, which is another argument for a short, well-prioritised programme.

Skin irritation, sleep disruption and the social weight of wearing a brace at school are all real and worth raising with the prescribing team. They are common reasons wear time quietly drops, and they are often fixable.

What is not settled

Whether adding scoliosis-specific exercise to bracing improves the final curve outcome, as opposed to improving strength, mobility and how people cope, has not been definitively established.
