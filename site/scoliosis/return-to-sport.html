Pillar 03 · Rayhab's approach

Returning to sport with a known curve

Most people with scoliosis can play most sports. Where it goes wrong is stopping entirely, or returning without a plan.

Reading time2 min read
Written byRayan Halimeh, Certified Schroth Practitioner
StatusAwaiting clinical review

In short

  • Sport is generally encouraged. Withdrawal from activity carries its own cost.
  • Return is decided by criteria, not by a number of weeks.
  • Asymmetric sports are not banned. They are balanced with deliberate work on the other side.
  • The corrective programme continues through the season, usually in a reduced form.

The default is to keep playing

There is no good evidence that a curve makes ordinary sport unsafe, and the costs of stopping are real: lost fitness, lost strength, lost social contact, and a teenager who now thinks of themselves as fragile.

That last one is the most expensive and the least discussed.

Criteria rather than a calendar

If sport has been paused, for pain, a flare, or a period of intensive treatment, return should be decided by what someone can demonstrate, not by how many weeks have passed.

  • Can the corrective position be held under the loads the sport demands?
  • Is side-to-side capacity reasonably matched in the tests that matter for that sport?
  • Does the trunk hold up when fatigued, not just when fresh?
  • Do symptoms settle within a day after a hard session, rather than accumulating?

Asymmetric sports

Tennis, swimming with a dominant breathing side, throwing sports, fencing, golf and rowing all load one side differently. Families sometimes ask whether these should be stopped.

Usually not. The evidence that asymmetric sport drives curve progression is weak. The practical answer is to keep the sport and deliberately train the other side, so the total loading across the week is less lopsided than the sport alone.

Through a season

The corrective programme does not stop for the season; it shrinks. A reduced daily version that survives training and travel is far more valuable than a full programme suspended for four months and restarted badly.

Plan the in-season version before the season starts, while there is still attention available to plan it.
