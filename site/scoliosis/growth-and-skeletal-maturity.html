Pillar 01 · What scoliosis is

Growth, Risser sign and why timing matters so much

If you understand one thing about adolescent scoliosis, make it this: the clock matters more than the number.

Reading time2 min read
Written byRayan Halimeh, Certified Schroth Practitioner
StatusAwaiting clinical review

In short

  • Progression in idiopathic scoliosis is driven mainly by growth.
  • Skeletal maturity is estimated from X-ray signs and growth milestones, not from age alone.
  • The Risser sign grades maturity from 0 to 5 using a growth plate on the pelvis.
  • The window where active treatment can do most is also the window that closes.
  • Once growth is complete, the situation becomes considerably more stable.

Why timing dominates

Idiopathic curves change most during rapid growth. The practical implication is uncomfortable but important: the period when a curve is most likely to worsen is also the period when intervention has the most to work with, and that period is finite.

This is why clinicians sometimes want to review a growing adolescent every few months while being relaxed about an adult with a similar curve.

The Risser sign

The Risser sign grades how far a growth plate along the top of the pelvis has ossified and fused, on a scale from 0 to 5. It is visible on the same standing X-ray used to measure the curve, so it costs nothing extra.

  • Risser 0 means that growth plate has not begun to ossify. Considerable growth typically remains.
  • Risser 1 to 3 covers the progressive stages, spanning the fastest part of the growth spurt.
  • Risser 4 to 5 indicates growth is largely or entirely complete.

Risser is a useful shorthand rather than a precise clock, and it is read alongside other information.

The other markers

  • Height velocity, how fast someone is currently growing, tracked across visits.
  • Menarche, since the growth rate usually slows in the period following it.
  • Hand and wrist films or other bone-age methods, used where a more precise estimate is needed.

What this means for a family

If a curve is found early in the growth spurt, expect closer monitoring and a lower threshold for acting. That is not alarmism; it is making use of a window.

If a curve is found once growth is finished, the conversation shifts away from progression and towards symptoms, function, appearance and long-term management. Both are legitimate reasons to do rehabilitation. They are just different reasons.
