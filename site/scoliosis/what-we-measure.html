Pillar 04 · The assessment

What we measure, and what we re-measure

Rehabilitation without measurement becomes a matter of opinion, and opinion is a poor guide over years.

Reading time2 min read
Written byDr. Rayan Halimeh, Certified Schroth Practitioner
StatusAwaiting clinical review

In short

  • Measurements are taken at baseline and repeated at defined review points.
  • Trunk rotation, posture, mobility, strength and function are all tracked.
  • The Cobb angle comes from imaging and is not re-measured at every review.
  • Some things will not have changed, and the record says so.

Why measure at all

Change in scoliosis is slow. Over months, perception drifts: a good day feels like progress, a sore week feels like deterioration, and nobody can reliably recall what their shoulder looked like in March.

A record removes that. It also protects you from a clinic telling you things are improving when they are not, which is the more important reason.

What gets measured

  • Trunk rotation in degrees, using a scoliometer at defined levels.
  • Standing posture, photographed against a grid with consent, in a repeatable position.
  • Trunk balance, how the head and trunk sit over the pelvis.
  • Mobility, spinal and hip range relevant to the corrections being trained.
  • Strength and endurance, particularly the ability to hold a correction over time and under load.
  • Breathing, chest expansion and the ability to direct breath into the collapsed regions.
  • Function, the tasks you specifically came in unable to tolerate.

Where the Cobb angle fits

The Cobb angle comes from imaging and therefore is not repeated at every review. It is tracked when imaging is done for clinical reasons, and interpreted with measurement error in mind: a change of a few degrees between films may be noise rather than progression.

When it is repeated

Review intervals depend on situation. A growing adolescent in a period of rapid change is reviewed more often than an adult managing symptoms. The interval is set at the outset so the review is planned rather than prompted by worry.

Reporting it honestly

Some reviews show improvement. Some show no change. Some, particularly through a growth spurt, show a curve that has progressed despite everything being done properly.

All three get reported as they are. A record that only ever contains good news is not a record, and the point of measuring is to make it possible to change course when something is not working.
