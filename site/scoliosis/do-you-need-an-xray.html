Pillar 04 · The assessment

Do you need an X-ray before your first visit?

This question delays a lot of first appointments unnecessarily, so here is the direct answer.

Reading time2 min read
Written byRayan Halimeh, Schroth Level 2
StatusAwaiting clinical review

In short

  • You do not need an X-ray to be assessed. Bring one if you already have it.
  • An X-ray gives the Cobb angle and skeletal maturity, which nothing else provides.
  • Trunk rotation, posture, strength and mobility are all assessable without imaging.
  • Imaging is requested when it would change the plan, not routinely.

The direct answer

No. The assessment goes ahead without imaging, and you will be told honestly afterwards whether an X-ray would change anything. Waiting several weeks to arrange one before being seen usually costs more than it gains.

What an X-ray does provide

  • The Cobb angle, which cannot be obtained any other way and which treatment thresholds are built on.
  • Skeletal maturity, via the Risser sign, which is central to judging progression risk.
  • Curve structure, including whether a curve is structural or compensatory.
  • A baseline for later comparison, if one does not already exist.

That is a substantial list, which is why imaging is often appropriate at some point in a growing adolescent. It is not a reason to obtain one before a first conversation.

What can be assessed without it

A great deal: trunk rotation with a scoliometer, curve pattern and direction, posture and trunk balance, mobility, strength, endurance, breathing, and how you move under load. That is enough to start a programme and enough to judge whether onward referral is needed.

The radiation question

A spinal X-ray is a small dose, but adolescents with scoliosis may be imaged repeatedly over years, so the total matters and is worth minimising sensibly. Modern imaging protocols and low-dose systems have reduced exposure considerably.

The reasonable position is neither to avoid imaging nor to repeat it reflexively: image when the result would change a decision, and use the longest interval consistent with safe monitoring.

If you already have imaging

Bring it, including older films if you have them. A sequence of measurements over time is considerably more informative than any single image, because it shows direction of travel rather than a snapshot.

Please do not email imaging or reports before being told how to share them securely.
